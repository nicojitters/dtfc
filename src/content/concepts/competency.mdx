## What is a Competency?

A **competency** is the combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, behaviors, and experience that enable someone to be authentic and effective in a wide variety of situations. It is broader than just having a skill — it includes knowing when and how to apply that skill appropriately in real situations.

A "skill" is a specific learned ability (like articulating consonants clearly, or maintaining physical stillness) that is measurable. A "competency" encompasses the skill plus the judgment, motivation, and situational awareness to use it effectively.

For example, "communication" as a competency includes not just the skills of speaking or writing clearly, but also knowing when to listen, how to adapt a message to different audiences, and enough context awareness to recognize what is happening — or required — in any situation and respond appropriately.

The distinction between "competency" and "skill" in professional practice emerged in the 1970s, largely through the work of psychologist David McClelland at Harvard. In 1973, McClelland published "Testing for Competence Rather Than for Intelligence," arguing that traditional measures like IQ tests and academic credentials were poor predictors of real-world performance.

## The Five DT:FC Competencies

Developmental Theatre: Fearless Creativity trains five natural competencies that enable individuals, teams, and organizations to provide creative approaches to what is needed in any current situation.

- **Physical Expression** — Entry, Movement, Mime, Rhythm
- **Vocal Expression** — Expression, Articulation, Finding a Voice, Storytelling
- **Context Awareness** — the exploration of where each person is, what is going on there, and how to act inside that situation
- **Risk Assessment and Management** — how to assess risk and then manage it. Fearless Creativity is born here; it's not Foolish Creativity.
- **Resilience** — the ability to replace "failure" with "learning," to get up and do it again, or explore a different direction

These competencies are explored through Theatre Games and referenced throughout the DT:FC website.
