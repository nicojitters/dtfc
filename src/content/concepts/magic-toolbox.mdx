## What is the Magic Toolbox?

A Theatre Games facilitator and players need only one thing: an open space. However, it can be helpful to have a **Magic Toolbox** — a small, colorful box or bag that stores items that make games easier to coordinate. Check each theatre game in advance to see if any specific equipment is needed.

A director’s Magic Toolbox may be a sturdy physical box that can be opened and sat or stood on. Other than the Toolbox itself, set pieces and props are mimed — and players' memory maintains the size, shape, and place of each imagined object. If the audience has imagined a door, they will notice if it moves.

## The Essential Tool: Open Space

The only tool you must have is an open space large enough for all players to practice an activity at the same time. It should be a space where loud noise is possible — articulation exercises, group vocalizations, and physical games require it. Involving participants in creating that space at the start of each session can itself be a ritualized warmup.

For online sessions, each person creates their own open space at their location.

## Helpful Contents

**Music making:**
- A small drum
- A flute or whistle
- Lummi sticks or claves (wooden sticks that produce a sharp, ringing sound when struck together)
- A source for recorded music (charged and ready)

**Partner selection:**
- A standard deck of playing cards allows immediate random grouping. Drawing a single card divides a group into four (hearts, spades, clubs, diamonds). Pre-selecting cards by number creates pairs, trios, or quartets.
- Colored cardboard squares (yellow, green, red, blue) for any division of the group into smaller ones.

*Do not put in the Toolbox:* costume items like hats, fabric pieces that could be worn, or sharp objects.

## Private Magic Toolboxes

Each player may develop a private, mental Magic Toolbox — contents known only to them. The group can also play a theatre game of *being* the objects inside the Magic Toolbox.
