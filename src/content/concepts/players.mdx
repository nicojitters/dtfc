## Who Are Players?

In Developmental Theatre: Fearless Creativity, everyone who participates in a theatre game is called a **Player** — never an "actor." This is a deliberate choice of vocabulary.

"Actor" carries connotations of performance, expertise, and evaluation by an audience. "Player" carries connotations of exploration, participation, and creative freedom. Every person in the room — regardless of experience, age, ability, or confidence — is a Player from the moment they join.

## Why It Matters

The word "player" removes the hierarchy that the word "actor" introduces. In DT:FC, there is no requirement to perform well, to have prior training, or to produce something polished. There is only the requirement to participate — to say "Yes, and…" and see what happens next.

This matters practically: when a facilitator says "You're all players," it signals that the games are for everyone. Players can participate from a chair, can use a modified version of any gesture, and can contribute to the group even with physical or vocal differences. No player is treated differently from the rotation.

## Players and Ensemble

A group of Players working well together — with shared warmup, matching cohesion, and a skilled facilitator — becomes an **ensemble**. Ensemble is not a title or a level to achieve. It emerges from consistent, honest play over time.

DT:FC Theatre Games are designed so that Players at all levels of cohesion can experience ensemble moments from the very first session. Those moments — when the group moves together without being told to, or when laughter erupts from shared surprise — are the core reward of the work.
