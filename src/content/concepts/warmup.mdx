## What is a Warmup?

In the moment when a group gathers — for a class, session, meeting, or rehearsal — each person arrives with a different "warmup." Whatever happened to them before comes with them into the gathering space. One person had a delightful time; another was stressed on the way there; a third didn't get enough sleep.

**Physical readiness** is the idea of warming up the body rather than beginning strenuous activity immediately. The key components of a physical warmup are: pulse raising, mobility, stretching, dynamic movements, and skill rehearsal. Theatre Games often include all of these.

**Psychological readiness** is the idea that a person and a group can warm up the mind, heart, and spirit to be or work together. Everyone becomes aware of the context — where they are and who they are within this group. People warmed up to each other and the task accomplish more in shorter periods of time.

**Emotional readiness** occurs when players can recognize and adjust their own warmup to a person or situation — and recognize and respond to others' warmups. This trains the foundation of skillful relationship management.

## Using Warmup Theatre Games

Use a Warmup Theatre Game when a group first gathers. It can be:

1. The first time ever together — group getting to know each other.
2. The first time together today — creating cohesion quickly.

Even if everyone knows each other well, each person arrives from a different daily experience. The goal is to replace whatever individual warmup each player brings into the space by creating a **common warmup** for all participants to the present place, time, task, and people.

Most warmup games can be adapted for all age groups — from pre-K through senior citizens — and can be very short or expanded to take up substantial time.

## Cool-Down

A cool-down brings closure to the time together and creates energy for leaving the space. It can be as simple as standing together, taking five long breaths, waving at each other, or passing a pulse around the circle by hand. When the circle is complete, everyone breaks hands and leaves.
