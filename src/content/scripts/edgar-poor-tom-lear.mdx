## Production Notes

Edgar, dispossessed and hunted, has disguised himself as Poor Tom — a wandering bedlamite — and stumbles onto the storm-drenched heath where King Lear himself has been driven to the edge of madness. This speech is a performance within a performance: Edgar playing a character in extremis while himself in extremis.

The speech is prose, not verse, and deliberately fragmented. It rewards Players who can commit to physical expression — the speech describes a body that has been through fire, flood, and persecution. What Poor Tom&rsquo;s body has endured should be visible.

## Script

**EDGAR**

Who gives anything to Poor Tom, whom the foul fiend hath led through fire and through flame, through ford and whirlpool, o&rsquo;er bog and quagmire; that hath laid knives under his pillow and halters in his pew, set ratsbane by his porridge, made him proud of heart to ride on a bay trotting horse over four-inched bridges to course his own shadow for a traitor? Bless thy five wits! Tom&rsquo;s a-cold. O, do de, do de, do de. Bless thee from whirlwinds, star-blasting, and taking! Do Poor Tom some charity, whom the foul fiend vexes. There could I have him now, and there — and there again — and there.

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Explore the body in discomfort — cold, wet, shaking — without performing it. Ask Players to find physical specificity first, then let the text follow.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Edgar is performing madness while experiencing genuine danger and grief. Where in the speech does Edgar-the-actor seem to break through Poor Tom-the-role?
- The repetition of "and there — and there again — and there" — what is happening in that moment? What does Tom seem to be tracking?
- This speech is begging. How does that change a Player&rsquo;s relationship to their audience?
- "Tom&rsquo;s a-cold" — three words in the middle of a long performance. What do those words do?
