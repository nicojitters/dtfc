## Production Notes

Edmund opens with a declaration of allegiance to Nature over law — to appetite, energy, and cunning over the accident of birth order. He is a bastard son who intends to take what the world has withheld from him. The speech is a manifesto delivered with full wit and charm.

Edmund is one of Shakespeare&rsquo;s most attractive villains precisely because his argument is not entirely wrong. Players who find the genuine grievance underneath the scheming will give the speech real power.

## Script

**EDMUND**

Thou, Nature, art my goddess. To thy law
My services are bound. Wherefore should I
Stand in the plague of custom, and permit
The curiosity of nations to deprive me
For that I am some twelve or fourteen moonshines
Lag of a brother? why "bastard"? Wherefore "base,"
When my dimensions are as well compact,
My mind as generous and my shape as true
As honest madam&rsquo;s issue? Why brand they us
With "base," with "baseness," "bastardy," "base," "base,"
Who, in the lusty stealth of nature, take
More composition and fierce quality
Than doth within a dull, stale, tired bed
Go to th&rsquo; creating a whole tribe of fops
Got &rsquo;tween asleep and wake? Well then,
Legitimate Edgar, I must have your land.
Our father&rsquo;s love is to the bastard Edmund
As to th&rsquo; legitimate. Fine word, "legitimate."
Well, my legitimate, if this letter speed
And my invention thrive, Edmund the base
Shall top th&rsquo; legitimate. I grow, I prosper.
Now, gods, stand up for bastards!

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Ask Players: what is something you have every right to want but were told you can&rsquo;t have? Approach the speech from that energy.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Edmund repeats "legitimate" several times. What does the word mean to him? How does his voice change each time he says it?
- The speech is addressed to Nature — a goddess. How does speaking to an invisible presence change the way a Player delivers the argument?
- Edmund ends by asking the gods to stand up for bastards. Does he believe they will? Does he need them to?
- Where do Players feel sympathy for Edmund? Where does that sympathy become uncomfortable?
