## Production Notes

In the middle of a battle he cannot control and does not want, King Henry VI sits on a hillock and imagines the life he would rather have: a shepherd&rsquo;s life of simple, measurable hours. The speech is a sustained act of grief — not for any one loss, but for the life a king is never permitted to live.

Henry&rsquo;s meditation moves from envy of the shepherd to a careful arithmetic of time (hours, days, weeks, years) before landing on a final, devastating comparison between royal luxury and shepherd&rsquo;s peace. The repetition is the point: it is the speech of a man counting what he cannot have.

## Script

**KING HENRY**

Would I were dead, if God&rsquo;s good will were so,
For what is in this world but grief and woe?
O God! Methinks it were a happy life
To be no better than a homely swain,
To sit upon a hill as I do now,
To carve out dials quaintly, point by point,
Thereby to see the minutes how they run:
How many makes the hour full complete,
How many hours brings about the day,
How many days will finish up the year,
How many years a mortal man may live.
When this is known, then to divide the times:
So many hours must I tend my flock,
So many hours must I take my rest,
So many hours must I contemplate,
So many hours must I sport myself,
So many days my ewes have been with young,
So many weeks ere the poor fools will ean,
So many years ere I shall shear the fleece;
So minutes, hours, days, months, and years,
Passed over to the end they were created,
Would bring white hairs unto a quiet grave.
Ah, what a life were this! How sweet, how lovely!
Gives not the hawthorn bush a sweeter shade
To shepherds looking on their silly sheep
Than doth a rich embroidered canopy
To kings that fear their subjects&rsquo; treachery?
O yes, it doth, a thousandfold it doth.
And to conclude, the shepherd&rsquo;s homely curds,
His cold thin drink out of his leather bottle,
His wonted sleep under a fresh tree&rsquo;s shade,
All which secure and sweetly he enjoys,
Is far beyond a prince&rsquo;s delicates —
His viands sparkling in a golden cup,
His body couchèd in a curious bed —
When care, mistrust, and treason waits on him.

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Ask Players to imagine an alternative life in concrete, specific terms — not abstract happiness, but particular hours and tasks. That specificity is what the speech requires.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Henry&rsquo;s speech is structured as arithmetic — "so many hours," "so many days." Why does a grief speech count things?
- The shepherd and the king are compared by what each can control. What does Henry believe kings cannot control?
- Henry begins with "Would I were dead." The speech does not end in despair. Where does it go instead, and why?
- What does this speech ask of an audience? Is it asking for sympathy, judgment, or something else?
