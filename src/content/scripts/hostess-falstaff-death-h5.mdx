## Production Notes

The Hostess — Mistress Quickly — describes the death of Falstaff to his old companions. Falstaff never appears in *Henry V*; he is already gone when the play begins, and the Hostess&rsquo;s speech is his entire death scene. It is a masterpiece of comic-grief: funny and heartbreaking in the same breath, as specific and unsentimental as a nurse&rsquo;s report.

The speech has no self-pity. The Hostess describes what she saw and felt with complete accuracy and only intermittent awareness of what it meant. Players who play the observation — not the emotion about the observation — will find the grief arrives on its own.

## Script

**HOSTESS**

He&rsquo;s in Arthur&rsquo;s bosom, if ever man went to Arthur&rsquo;s bosom. He made a finer end, and went away an it had been any christom child. He parted ev&rsquo;n just between twelve and one, ev&rsquo;n at the turning o&rsquo; th&rsquo; tide; for after I saw him fumble with the sheets and play with flowers and smile upon his finger&rsquo;s end, I knew there was but one way, for his nose was as sharp as a pen and he talked of green fields. "How now, Sir John?" quoth I. "What, man, be o&rsquo; good cheer!" So he cried out "God, God, God!" three or four times. Now I, to comfort him, bid him he should not think of God; I hoped there was no need to trouble himself with any such thoughts yet. So he bade me lay more clothes on his feet. I put my hand into the bed and felt them, and they were as cold as any stone. Then I felt to his knees, and so upward and upward, and all was as cold as any stone.

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Ask Players to describe something they have witnessed — something that mattered — in the most precise factual terms they can. Then bring that register to the speech.

**Debrief prompts:**
- "I bid him he should not think of God; I hoped there was no need to trouble himself with any such thoughts yet." This line is often played for a laugh. What does it reveal about the Hostess?
- The Hostess feels his body going cold — "so upward and upward." What does that physical act of checking tell us about her relationship with him?
- Falstaff is one of Shakespeare&rsquo;s greatest characters. His death is reported, not shown. What does it mean to give his death speech to someone else?
- The speech ends on the physical: "all was as cold as any stone." Why does Shakespeare end here, with this image?
