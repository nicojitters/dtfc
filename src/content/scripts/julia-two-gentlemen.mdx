## Production Notes

Julia, disguised as the page Sebastian, has just been given a message to deliver to Silvia from the man she herself loves — Proteus. In this brief speech she holds Silvia&rsquo;s picture and measures herself against her rival. The comedy of disguise curdles into something genuinely painful: Julia is an audience to her own displacement.

Excellent for Players exploring the gap between what a character says and what a character feels. The speech is conversational in register but the stakes beneath it are acute.

## Script

**JULIA**

Alas, how love can trifle with itself!
Here is her picture; let me see. I think
If I had such a tire, this face of mine
Were full as lovely as is this of hers;
And yet the painter flattered her a little,
Unless I flatter with myself too much.
Her hair is auburn; mine is perfect yellow;
If that be all the difference in his love,
I’ll get me such a colored periwig.
Her eyes are gray as glass, and so are mine.
Ay, but her forehead&rsquo;s low, and mine&rsquo;s as high.
What should it be that he respects in her
But I can make respective in myself
If this fond Love were not a blinded god?

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Try a mirroring game — one Player leads small physical adjustments while the other copies. Then flip: who is the original?

**Debrief prompts:**
- Julia lists her features against Silvia&rsquo;s one by one. Where does her confidence slip? Where does she rally it?
- The speech ends with a question, not a conclusion. What does Julia decide not to decide?
- What does it mean to play a scene where you are performing for yourself? Where is the audience in this speech?
- How does knowing Julia&rsquo;s full situation change the way a Player might deliver "Alas, how love can trifle with itself"?
