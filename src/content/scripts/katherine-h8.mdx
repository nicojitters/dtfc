## Production Notes

Queen Katherine, on trial for her marriage to Henry VIII, turns directly on Cardinal Wolsey. She refuses to accept him as her judge, calling out the gap between his outward piety and his inward arrogance with precise, controlled fury. It is not a breakdown — it is a legal argument delivered at white heat.

Katherine is one of Shakespeare&rsquo;s most dignified characters under pressure. The speech rewards Players who can keep the anger cold, not hot. Every word is chosen. She is not losing control; she is exercising it.

## Script

**QUEEN KATHERINE**

You&rsquo;re meek and humble-mouthed;
You sign your place and calling, in full seeming,
With meekness and humility, but your heart
Is crammed with arrogancy, spleen, and pride.
You have by fortune and his Highness&rsquo; favors
Gone slightly o&rsquo;er low steps, and now are mounted
Where powers are your retainers, and your words,
Domestics to you, serve your will as &rsquo;t please
Yourself pronounce their office. I must tell you,
You tender more your person&rsquo;s honor than
Your high profession spiritual, that again
I do refuse you for my judge.

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Explore precision — the difference between controlled fury and uncontrolled rage. Ask Players to deliver a strong statement as quietly as possible, then try the speech.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Katherine&rsquo;s speech is short but devastating. How does she structure her accusation? What comes first, what comes last, and why?
- "I do refuse you for my judge" is her conclusion. What does it mean to refuse a judge in a trial? What risk is Katherine taking?
- Wolsey outranks Katherine in the Church&rsquo;s hierarchy. How does she dismantle that hierarchy in the speech?
- Where is the grief in this speech? It&rsquo;s classified as grief — but where does it live?
