## Production Notes

<p class="scenes-possible-cut-legend">Highlighted spans mark optional cuts.</p>

The wooing scene. Try it with high stakes and no eye contact for the first run-through. Then try it with constant eye contact, almost aggressive intimacy. The Players will discover which version honors the text.

## Script

**PETRUCHIO:**

Good morrow, Kate; for that's your name, I hear.

**KATHARINA:**

Well have you heard, but something hard of hearing:
They call me Katharine that do talk of me.

**PETRUCHIO:**

You lie, in faith; for you are call'd plain Kate,
<span class="possible-cut">And bonny Kate and sometimes Kate the curst;</span>
But Kate, the prettiest Kate in Christendom,
Kate of Padua.

**KATHARINA:**

Patience, I pray you; 'twas a fault unwilling.

**PETRUCHIO:**

Nay, hear you, Kate: in sooth you scape not so.

**KATHARINA:**

I chafe you, if I tarry: let me go.

**PETRUCHIO:**

No, not a whit: I find you passing gentle.
'Twas told me you were rough and coy and sullen,
And now I find report a very liar;
For thou art pleasant, gamesome, passing courteous,
But slow in speech, yet sweet as spring-time flowers:
Thou canst not frown, thou canst not look askance,
Nor bite the lip, as angry wenches will,
<span class="possible-cut">Nor hast thou colour'd passion like to thine.</span>

But thou with mildness entertain'st thy wooers,
With gentle conference, soft and affable.
Why, does the world report that Kate doth limp?
Is it a lie that reports so?

**KATHARINA:**

I pray you, sir, is it your will
To make a stale of me amongst these mates?

**PETRUCHIO:**

Why, here's no crab; and therefore welcome.

**KATHARINA:**

Nay, good sign, though you hit not me; yet, as the manner is, I cry a match.

**PETRUCHIO:**

<span class="possible-cut">Why, thou hast hit it:</span> I am sure, sweet Kate,
This is a very venerable cover for thy head;
An 'twere of gold or silver, sure 'twould make
Thee look exceeding proper in thy new attire.

**KATHARINA:**

Thy head! thy head!

**PETRUCHIO:**

Ay, upon my life I swear it. See, where comes your father.

*Enter BAPTISTA with GREMIO and TRANIO.*

**BAPTISTA:**

Now, Signior Petruchio, how speed you with my daughter?

**PETRUCHIO:**

How but well, sir? how but well?
It were impossible I should speed amiss.

**BAPTISTA:**

Why, how now, daughter Katharina!

**KATHARINA:**

<span class="possible-cut">Call you me daughter? now, I promise you,
You have show'd a tender fatherly regard,
To wish me wed to one half mad;</span>
But yet I never said I wished for you:
Nay, God's my judge, you came not into the list
Of my affections at the first;
Nor can I now repent me of my choice.

**PETRUCHIO:**

Why, and I told you before, Katharina,
I am as peremptory as she proud-minded;
And where two raging fires meet together
They do consume the thing that feeds their fury:
Though little fire grows great with little wind,
Yet extreme gusts will blow out fire and all:
So I to her and so she yields to me;
For I am rough and woo not like a babe.

**KATHARINA:**

<span class="possible-cut">Mates,</span> I'll cuff you, if you strike again.

**PETRUCHIO:**

So come to me and be merry.

**GREMIO:**

I knew a woman in Florence, Signior Petruchio, that had a scold's bridle for her tongue, longer than a great man's beard; and yet she sent word, she died a shrew. But I think he 'scaped with wearing out her tongue.

**PETRUCHIO:**

Ay, 'twere a kind punishment, for he began first to scold and rail at me; therefore fairly, good Katharina, we will unto your father's even in the night-time, and there will I, in the tongueless grove, make love to you so pleasant and so merrily as no man ever made before.

**KATHARINA:**

I will not to your father's.

**PETRUCHIO:**

Nay; but you shall come, sweet Katharina; for we will have rings and things and fine array; and kiss me, Kate, we will be married o' Sunday.

---

*Exeunt PETRUCHIO and KATHARINA separately.*

## Facilitator Notes

The possible-cut spans in yellow are optional. Players choose whether to include or drop each one. Compare two complete performances: one with all cuts included, one with none. How does the rhythm change? The comedy? The emotional stakes? This scene rewards Players who find the balance between Petruchio&rsquo;s aggressive confidence and Katharina&rsquo;s sharp intelligence — they are equals trading wit.
