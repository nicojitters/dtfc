## Production Notes

Petruchio announces his strategy for wooing Katherine before she enters: he will contradict everything she says or does, turning her sharpest weapons against her. The speech is a comic battle plan, delivered with the confidence of a man who believes he can out-maneuver anyone.

Great for Players working with physical comedy and comic timing. The speech is rhythmically tight — each "Say that she…" clause a new move in a game. Let Players feel the mounting absurdity without losing Petruchio&rsquo;s genuine self-assurance.

## Script

**PETRUCHIO**

I&rsquo;ll woo her with some spirit when she comes!
Say that she rail, why then I&rsquo;ll tell her plain
She sings as sweetly as a nightingale.
Say that she frown, I&rsquo;ll say she looks as clear
As morning roses newly washed with dew.
Say she be mute and will not speak a word,
Then I&rsquo;ll commend her volubility
And say she uttereth piercing eloquence.
If she do bid me pack, I&rsquo;ll give her thanks
As though she bid me stay by her a week.
If she deny to wed, I&rsquo;ll crave the day
When I shall ask the banns, and when be marrièd.
But here she comes — and now, Petruchio, speak.

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Play a game of opposites — one Player says or does something, the partner responds with the opposite and frames it as a compliment. Then try the speech.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Petruchio names each of Katherine&rsquo;s rumored qualities — loud, sharp, unyielding — and treats them as assets. What does his strategy assume about her?
- The final line is a private instruction to himself: "now, Petruchio, speak." What does that moment of self-coaching reveal about his actual confidence level?
- Is this a villain speech or a hero speech? Where do Players land, and why?
- How does the speech change if we imagine Katherine is already listening from offstage?
