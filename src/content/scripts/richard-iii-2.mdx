## Production Notes

On the eve of Bosworth Field, Richard wakes from a nightmare in which every ghost of every person he has murdered has cursed him. This speech is the result: a fractured, self-interrogating unraveling in which Richard tries to locate his conscience and finds it entirely intact — and entirely condemning. It is his most human moment.

The speech is extraordinary for the internal debate it stages — Richard literally argues with himself, asking and answering his own questions. Players who can make each turn feel like a genuine discovery — not a performance of confusion — will find its full power.

## Script

**KING RICHARD** *(starting up out of a dream)*

Give me another horse! Bind up my wounds!
Have mercy, Jesu! — Soft, I did but dream.
O coward conscience, how dost thou afflict me!
The lights burn blue; it is now dead midnight.
Cold fearful drops stand on my trembling flesh.
What do I fear? Myself? There&rsquo;s none else by.
Richard loves Richard, that is, I am I.
Is there a murderer here? No. Yes, I am.
Then fly! What, from myself? Great reason why:
Lest I revenge. What, myself upon myself?
Alack, I love myself. Wherefore? For any good
That I myself have done unto myself?
O, no. Alas, I rather hate myself
For hateful deeds committed by myself.
I am a villain. Yet I lie; I am not.
Fool, of thyself speak well. Fool, do not flatter.
My conscience hath a thousand several tongues,
And every tongue brings in a several tale,
And every tale condemns me for a villain.
Perjury, perjury, in the highest degree;
Murder, stern murder, in the direst degree;
All several sins, all used in each degree,
Throng to the bar, crying all "Guilty, guilty!"
I shall despair. There is no creature loves me,
And if I die no soul will pity me.
And wherefore should they, since that I myself
Find in myself no pity to myself?

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Ask Players to name something they&rsquo;ve done that they&rsquo;d prefer not to think about. Then explore the body&rsquo;s response to self-accusation — not guilt as an idea, but as a physical state.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Richard asks "Is there a murderer here?" and immediately answers himself. What does it mean that he cannot lie to himself, even though he has lied to everyone else?
- "There is no creature loves me." Is this self-pity, factual observation, or both?
- The speech starts in a half-waking state and ends in full clarity. Where is the turn?
- Players often discover that Richard&rsquo;s funniest moments and his most broken moment are this speech. How does that coexist?
