## Production Notes

Romeo, believing Juliet dead, stands over her body in the Capulet tomb. The speech moves through wonder, grief, rage, and final resolution — he cannot understand why she still looks so alive, and that beauty is what undoes him. He drinks the poison in the last line and dies on a kiss.

This is one of Shakespeare&rsquo;s longest death speeches. The structure is a series of addresses — to men generally, to Tybalt&rsquo;s ghost, to Juliet herself, to Death, to his own body — each transition requiring a new physicality. Players who can genuinely look at their playing partner and be surprised by what they see will find the speech&rsquo;s emotional engine.

## Script

**ROMEO**

How oft when men are at the point of death
Have they been merry, which their keepers call
A light&rsquo;ning before death! O, how may I
Call this a light&rsquo;ning? — O my love, my wife,
Death, that hath sucked the honey of thy breath,
Hath had no power yet upon thy beauty.
Thou art not conquered. Beauty&rsquo;s ensign yet
Is crimson in thy lips and in thy cheeks,
And death&rsquo;s pale flag is not advancèd there. —
Tybalt, liest thou there in thy bloody sheet?
O, what more favor can I do to thee
Than with that hand that cut thy youth in twain
To sunder his that was thine enemy?
Forgive me, cousin. — Ah, dear Juliet,
Why art thou yet so fair? Shall I believe
That unsubstantial death is amorous,
And that the lean abhorrèd monster keeps
Thee here in dark to be his paramour?
For fear of that I still will stay with thee
And never from this palace of dim night
Depart again. Here, here will I remain
With worms that are thy chambermaids. O, here
Will I set up my everlasting rest
And shake the yoke of inauspicious stars
From this world-wearied flesh! Eyes, look your last.
Arms, take your last embrace. And, lips, O, you
The doors of breath, seal with a righteous kiss
A dateless bargain to engrossing death. *(Kissing Juliet.)*
Come, bitter conduct, come, unsavory guide!
Thou desperate pilot, now at once run on
The dashing rocks thy seasick weary bark!
Here&rsquo;s to my love. *(Drinking.)* O true apothecary,
Thy drugs are quick. Thus with a kiss I die. *(He dies.)*

## Facilitator Notes

**Warm-up:** Ask Players to look at something they love — a person, a photo — and describe it in specific detail, present tense. That capacity to see and report is the engine of Romeo&rsquo;s speech.

**Debrief prompts:**
- Romeo notices that Juliet still looks alive and is confused. How does the Player handle a character who sees something the audience knows is wrong?
- The speech addresses at least four different presences — other men, Tybalt, Juliet, Death itself. How does the Player mark each shift?
- Romeo says he will "set up his everlasting rest" — a term from gambling: all in. What does that metaphor do to the speech&rsquo;s final movement?
- The last image is a kiss. What does the physical act require of the Player in this moment?
